What to notice

Transverse: each particle moves perpendicular to the direction the wave travels. Examples: a wave on a string, light and other electromagnetic waves, S waves in an earthquake.

Longitudinal: each particle moves along the direction the wave travels, so the particles bunch up (compressions, C) and spread out (rarefactions, R). Examples: sound, a pushed Slinky, P waves.

s(x, t) = A sin(kx − ωt),   v = λ/T = fλ = ω/k

Both bands use the same s(x, t). Turn the longitudinal displacement 90° and you get the transverse picture: the bottom graph is that picture for both.

A compression is not at a crest of the graph. It sits where s crosses zero going down, because there the particle on the left has moved right and the particle on the right has moved left. The relative density is 1 − ∂s/∂x (to first order), a quarter wavelength from the displacement peaks.

Freeze the wave and step by T/8: the tracked particle returns to the same place after T, while the pattern has moved one wavelength.
